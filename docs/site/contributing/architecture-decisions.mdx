An ADR records a decision and the reasoning behind it, so that the constraint can
be re-examined later on its merits rather than rediscovered as folklore.

## Decisions with their own page

These constrain code outside Core as much as inside it, so they are written out in
full where the people they affect will look:

<Note>
  The register is not contiguous — numbers absent from it belong to decisions that
  were superseded and archived rather than published. An ADR that was replaced is
  kept in the repository with its successor named, because the reasoning that was
  overturned is part of why the current decision holds.
</Note>

## Writing one

An ADR is worth writing when a choice will constrain future work and the reasoning
would otherwise be lost — not for every technical decision. If the next person
would reasonably ask "why is it done this way, and may I change it", the answer
belongs in an ADR.

Record what was decided, what it rules out, and what would have to change for the
decision to be revisited. A decision without its alternatives is an instruction,
not a record.
