title: "Users"
description: "Inviting people and controlling their access."
---

Everyone who can sign in to this tenant, listed with their name, email, phone,
status, access (active or deactivated), and their roles. The search finds a user
by name, email or phone.

## Adding someone

**Invite user** asks for four things:

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | required |
| **Email** | required — this is what they sign in with |
| **Phone** | optional |
| **Role** | required — the role they start with; see [Roles and permissions](/using/roles-and-permissions) |

There is no password field. You are not creating credentials for someone else:
the account is created and the person sets their own password through an
activation link sent to the address you entered, valid for 72 hours. A row's
actions can send the activation email again.

<Note>
  This is deliberate and worth preserving as a habit. A password you typed is a
  password you know, and an account whose owner cannot honestly say only they
  could have used it is not much of an account.
</Note>

## Status and access

The list shows two separate columns, and they mean different things.

**Status** tracks the account's lifecycle — whether the invitation has been
accepted and the account completed.

**Access** is what you control, with **Activate** and **Deactivate** in the row's
actions. Deactivating signs the person out at once and blocks sign-in while
keeping the account, its roles, and everything it has done. That is what you want
when someone leaves or goes on extended leave: deleting the user would take their
history with them.

## Roles, not exceptions

A user is invited with one role and can be given more when edited; what they may
do is everything their roles allow together. There is no per-user exception to a
role's permissions.

That is what keeps access reviewable: to know what someone can do you look at
their roles, not at a role plus a list of adjustments. When somebody needs a
combination that no existing role covers, create the role — see
[Roles and permissions](/using/roles-and-permissions).

## Editing

**Edit** on a row changes the profile and the roles, and can set a new password
(left empty, the current one stays). Administrators give and take away any role;
everyone else only roles below their own, and roles out of their reach stay as
they are. Nobody changes their own roles. Changing roles takes effect on
the person's next request; they do not need to sign in again.

## Platform support

When the platform's support team helps you, they enter your tenant as one shared
account marked **Platform support** in the list. Nobody can edit, deactivate or
delete it, and it does not count towards your user limit.

Every such visit is recorded. **Support access**, next to Users in the admin
panel's menu, lists who from support entered, their email and IP,
and when they entered and left — newest first, and read only. It is open to anyone who can manage users.

## Limit notices

When a limit of the account (assistants, flows, channels, staff, active contacts, outgoing messages,
calls or media storage) turns work away, every active administrator gets a notification and an email
saying which limit it is, how much is used, what was turned away and who to ask for more. For limits
on how many things can exist, the same notice comes when the last free place is taken.

The email is written in the language the administrator last chose in the console. Until they choose one, it is the language they first signed in with; later sign-ins do not change it.
Each limit is announced once per billing period, or, for limits on how many things can exist and on
stored media, once for each limit value with a reminder every seven days while work keeps being turned
away. Other roles do not get these notices.
