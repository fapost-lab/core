Everything that belongs to one assistant lives in its own console: its channels,
its flows, the people it talks to, and the record of what it did.

You get there from the assistant list in the admin panel — **Manage** opens the
console, **View** stays in the admin panel and shows the record — or from the
**Console** in the application rail on the far left, which opens on the assistant
you last worked in. The assistant switcher opens another assistant's console, on
its dashboard. If you are looking for flows or conversations and cannot find them,
you are in the admin panel; see [The admin panel](/using/admin-overview).

## The sidebar

The console groups its screens by what you are doing rather than by what the data
is called.

| Group | Screens | For |
|---|---|---|
| **Overview** | Dashboard | Is it running, and is anything wrong |
| **Channels** | Channels | Where messages arrive from |
| **Flow** | Flows, Groups | What the assistant says and asks |
| **Operations** | Sessions, Flow logs, Contacts, Groups, Conversations, Segments, Broadcasts | The people, the traffic, and the record |
| **Settings** | Settings, Translations | How it behaves, and in which languages |

You see a screen only when your role lets you open it. **Conversations** carries
the number of threads with unread messages. <kbd>⌘K</kbd> (macOS) or
<kbd>Ctrl K</kbd> searches applications, pages and records from any screen — see
[Search](/using/admin-overview#search).

Operations is the largest group because it holds two different jobs that happen
to share a home: **working with people** — conversations, contacts, groups,
segments, broadcasts — and **finding out what happened** — sessions and flow
logs.

## Notifications

The bell in the header, in the console and the admin panel alike, collects the
notifications sent to you: in-app alerts from a flow's [Notify](/using/node-catalogue#notify) step in **Staff** mode and, for
administrators, notices that the organization reached a plan limit. The number on
the bell counts the unread ones and refreshes about once a minute. Open the bell to
see the latest twenty; choosing one marks it read and opens its link, if it has
one, and **Mark all as read** clears the count. You only ever see your own
notifications.

## The overview screen

The **Dashboard** opens on three panels (and a fourth, described below, when the assistant has turned people away), arranged in the order questions usually arrive:

**Summary** — the assistant's name and whether it is active. An inactive
assistant keeps everything it has but stops responding.

**Channels** — how many are connected, with a pointer to where they are managed.

**Contact limit** — shown only to people who may see contacts, and only when the plan's limit of monthly active contacts turned people away in the last 30 days. It says how many people and messages the bot did not answer and when it last happened.

**Operations** — two shortcuts that are really a health check: **Live sessions**
and **Errors 24 h**. Both are counters that should stay boring. Live sessions
rising without traffic to explain it means conversations are starting and not
finishing; errors above zero means at least one flow could not complete a step.

## Two assistants, two consoles

Each assistant has its own console at its own address, and nothing is shared
between them except what the tenant-wide [Settings](/using/tenant-settings)
define — languages, runtime limits, broadcast pacing.

Contacts are not shared. A person who writes to two assistants is two contacts,
because from each assistant's point of view they arrived independently.
