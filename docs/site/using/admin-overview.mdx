FaPost has two panels, and knowing which one you are in saves a lot of hunting.

| | Covers |
|---|---|
| **Admin panel** | The installation as a whole — settings, people, media, and the list of assistants |
| **Assistant console** | One assistant — its channels, flows, contacts, and conversations |

The admin panel is where you set things up. The console is where you work day to
day. Most of the user guide is about the console; this group is about everything
that sits above it.

## First sign-in: the setup wizard

A new tenant starts in a short setup wizard rather than on the dashboard. An
administrator confirms the content languages — the base language locks once the
first flow is published, and an assistant's language locks with its first flow —
and creates the first assistant. Until these required steps are done, the rest of
the panel is closed. Staff without the settings permission see a waiting page
("Your administrator is finishing the setup") until an administrator finishes.

Then the wizard offers two optional steps: connecting a channel and inviting the
team. Each can be skipped with **Skip for now** and done later, a channel in the
assistant's **Channels** menu and people under **Staff** → **Users**. After the last step the wizard lands in
the assistant's console. Tenants that existed before the wizard was introduced
never see it.

### The setup checklist

Once the wizard closes, administrators of a tenant that went through it see a
**Finish setting up** card on the dashboard and a setup checklist button in
the header, marked with the number of steps left. Both list every setup step — the optional ones too — with
what is done and a link to what is left, and disappear when everything is done.
**Hide** removes both for you only; the checklist comes back by itself when a
new setup step appears. Staff without the settings permission do not see it.

### A new required step after setup

An upgrade or a newly activated Solution can add a required setup step to a
tenant that has already finished. The tenant does not go back into the wizard:
everyone sees a banner above the page, "Setup needs another step", with a link
to it, until it is done. Administrators — anyone with the settings permission —
also get one notification in the bell when the step first appears; people who
join later see the banner only. Staff who cannot do the step see that their
administrator needs to finish it.

## The dashboard

Opening the panel lands you on a set of counters:

- **Assistants** — how many exist, how many are active
- **Contacts** — everyone the installation has ever talked to
- **Channels** — how many are configured and how many are connected
- **Published flows** — flows currently deployed and answering
- **Waiting sessions** — conversations paused mid-flow, awaiting a reply
- **Staff users** — people with access to this tenant

Below them, **Flow Activity** charts the last 14 days. Administrators also see a
**Limits and usage** card with the limits closest to being used up; see
[Usage and limits](/using/usage-and-limits).

You see a counter only if your role lets you open that kind of record — channels
need the permission to manage channels, staff users the permission to manage
users, and so on. A user whose role opens none of them sees an empty dashboard.
Administrators count the whole installation; everyone else counts only the
assistants assigned to them, so their channels, published flows, waiting sessions
and activity chart cover those assistants alone. Contacts and staff users are
always the installation's totals.

The figures refresh every minute while the page is open. After an hour they stop,
and a **Refresh** button brings them up to date and starts the hour again.

The counters are the fastest health check available. *Waiting sessions* climbing
without *published flows* changing usually means a flow asks a question nobody is
answering; *channels configured but not connected* means messages are arriving
nowhere.

## Switching applications

A narrow rail on the far left of every page holds the applications you work in:
the **Console** (one assistant at a time) and the **Admin panel**. Click one to go
there; the menu next to the rail belongs to the application you are in, and the
assistant switcher appears only in the Console. The Console opens on the assistant
you last worked in. You see the Console only when at least one assistant is open to
you.

The grid button at the bottom of the rail opens **All applications**: every
application you may open, with a search field. Pin or unpin an application there,
and move the pinned ones up or down to set their order in the rail. Pinning and
order are yours alone — other users keep their own. The application you are in
always shows in the rail, pinned or not.

## Search

Press <kbd>⌘K</kbd> (macOS) or <kbd>Ctrl K</kbd> on any page of either panel, or
click **Search all applications** in the header. Type at least two characters to
find:

- **Applications** by name, and **pages** — any menu entry of any application you
  may open (the Console's pages are those of the assistant you are looking at);
- **Records** — assistants, users, roles and media files by name (users by email
  too).

Each group shows its first few matches, and choosing one opens it. You only find
what your role lets you open — someone who sees only their assigned assistants
finds only those, and a page appears only when your menu shows it.

## What does not live here

Channels, flows, contacts, conversations, sessions and logs all belong to a
particular assistant, so they are not in this panel at all. Open the assistant
first — see [The assistant console](/using/console-overview).

The one exception is counting: the dashboard totals contacts and channels across
every assistant you may open, because that is a question about the installation
rather than about one of them.
