Every exchange between a contact and this assistant, newest activity first.

## The list

Each row summarises one conversation: which **contact**, over which **channel**,
the **last message**, how many are **unread**, the total **messages**, **who is
handling** it, and when it was **last active**.

The search finds a conversation by the contact's channel user ID, and the **Handling** filter
narrows the list: **Waiting for operator**, **Mine**, **With operators** or **Assistant**. Opening a row shows the full transcript. The list and an open conversation stay
current on their own: live when the install has a websocket broadcaster, otherwise
by refreshing every few seconds.

<Note>
  A contact is identified by the id the messaging platform gave them until the
  assistant learns something better — a name collected in a flow, for instance.
  A list of numbers is not a bug; it means those conversations have not reached
  a point where a name was asked for.
</Note>

## Unread and who is handling it

**Unread** counts inbound messages nobody has looked at. It is the queue for a
human, and it does not go down on its own.

A conversation is always handled by someone:

- **Assistant** — the flows answer the contact.
- **Waiting for an operator** — an operator was requested and nobody has accepted
  yet. The flows stand down; the contact's messages wait in the transcript.
- **Handled by** a named operator — that person answers, and only they can reply.

Opening a conversation marks it read. New messages that arrive while it is open
are counted until it is opened again.

<Note>
  A conversation and a [session](/using/sessions) are different things and are
  easy to confuse. The conversation is the whole relationship with that contact
  on that channel. A session is one pass through one flow. A conversation can
  have had many sessions, or none at all.
</Note>

## Replying

Where a person replies from — the operator inbox — the message goes out **under
the assistant's identity**. The contact sees the assistant, not the individual
typing.

Replying starts with **Take over** (on a conversation waiting for an operator the
button reads **Accept**): while you hold the conversation the flows stand down and
incoming messages wait for you. The first operator to take it wins — if another
operator already holds it, it stays theirs, and only the operator holding a
conversation can reply in it.

The operator holding a conversation can:

- **Finish** — hand it back to the assistant; the flows answer again.
- **Transfer** — hand it straight to another operator who works with this
  assistant and may read and reply to conversations.

An administrator can **take over from** another operator, for an escalation. To
reply, finish or transfer, an administrator takes the conversation first like
anyone else.

Each press of **Send** is one message. Pressing it twice, retrying, or
submitting the same draft again does not send it again; the inbox says the
reply was already sent. A draft written in another tab is a message of its own. If the inbox cannot confirm a reply went out — the
connection to the messenger broke mid-send — it says so and keeps your draft:
check the transcript before sending it again. An operator can send up to 30
replies a minute.

A reply counts against the tenant's outbound messages for the period. When they
are used up the reply is not sent, and the inbox says so instead of showing a
generic failure.

That is usually what you want, and it is exactly why the permission to do it is
marked sensitive: reading transcripts and speaking as the company are two
separate grants, and neither is routine. See
[Roles and permissions](/using/roles-and-permissions).

## When to read them

Transcripts answer the question flow logs cannot: *what did the person actually
want*. A flow that technically completed while the contact rephrased the same
question four times is working and failing at once, and only the transcript shows
it.

For the mechanical side — which node ran, what it returned, where execution
stopped — see [Flow logs](/using/flow-logs).
